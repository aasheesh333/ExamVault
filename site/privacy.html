Privacy policy

Last updated: 16 November 2026

ExamVault is a study utility for Indian competitive-exam aspirants. This document describes exactly what the app stores, what leaves your device, and what does not. It is written to match the code; where the two disagree, that is a bug.

The short version

  • There is no account. No email, phone number or password is ever collected.
  • Your admit cards, roll numbers and portal passwords are encrypted on your device and never uploaded.
  • Roll numbers you type are checked on your device and are not sent anywhere.
  • The app collects no analytics, no advertising identifiers, and no location.
  • If you allow notifications, the push providers (Firebase Cloud Messaging / OneSignal) hold a push token — a device identifier used only to route exam alerts for the categories you choose to your device. Details below, and in the Play "Data safety" answers at the end.

The encrypted store is excluded from Android Auto Backup and from device-to-device transfer (android:allowBackup="false" plus an explicit data-extraction rule), so your admit cards do not leave the phone even during a migration.

Deleting an admit card deletes its entry from the encrypted store.

Delete all data, in the app's About sheet, removes every admit card, every setting (including the notification categories you chose) and the cached feed and result index in one tap, without uninstalling. It also drops this device's push subscriptions — every per-exam bell and every category topic — and then re-subscribes the fresh-install default, which is all five categories, so the Notifications section in About shows what the device is actually subscribed to. The push token itself is kept — it carries no exam or vault data, and re-registering on every wipe would churn the provider for no privacy gain. Uninstalling the app removes everything, and the push token goes with it.

There is no way for us to recover your data. We never hold a copy of it.

What leaves your device

Three things, and nothing else:

  1. Plain HTTPS requests for public files. The app downloads a small JSON feed and, when you check a result, one gzip index. The request contains no personal data — no roll number, no device identifier, no cookie. It is the same request any browser would make for the same public file.
  2. A push-notification token, if you allow notifications. This token is a device identifier: it is issued by Firebase Cloud Messaging or OneSignal, and the provider uses it to route exam alerts to your device. The About sheet has a Notifications section where you choose which of the five exam categories (SSC, UPSC, Banking, Railways, State PSC) you follow. The token is subscribed only to the topics for the categories you chose, and turning one off unsubscribes it from that topic. Choosing no categories is allowed: you then receive no category alerts (per-exam bells you switched on separately are a different subscription and keep working), and the app says so on that screen. The token is not linked to your vault, your roll number, or any other data in the app, and it is deleted when you uninstall the app. Turning notifications off in Android settings stops alerts being shown on this device; it does not delete the token, which goes with the uninstall.
  3. Nothing else. No crash reports, no usage events, no device fingerprint. (The in-app error logger writes to the Dart VM's debug sink only; a release build has no such sink, and nothing is uploaded.)

Roll-number checks are local

To answer "is my roll in the list?", the app downloads a file containing only salted SHA-256 hashes of the published roll numbers. Your phone hashes the number you type and looks for a match on-device.

This means:

  • the number you type is never transmitted;
  • the file the app downloads cannot be turned back into a candidate list;
  • we do not learn which result you checked, or what you found.

The official result documents belong to the commissions. The app links to them on the commission's own website; it does not host or redistribute them.

Children

The app is intended for adults preparing for competitive examinations. It is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect data from them. The only identifier that ever leaves the device is the push token described above, which exists solely to route notifications and is not associated with any person by us.

There is no location, contacts, storage, camera or advertising permission, and no biometric permission — the vault has no biometric unlock; it is protected by device-level encryption alone.

Your choices

  • Notifications: deny the permission, or turn them off in Android settings — either stops alerts being shown on this device. There is no in-app master switch: the OS setting is the only way to silence notifications wholesale, and it does not delete the push token (only uninstalling does). The About sheet's Notifications section chooses which exam categories the token is subscribed to; turning every category off stops category alerts without changing the OS permission (per-exam bells are a separate subscription).
  • Your data: delete individual cards in the app, use Delete all data in About to remove every card and setting at once (it also drops the per-exam bells and the category topics, then re-applies the default of all five categories), or uninstall to remove everything.
  • Offline only: enable airplane mode. The feed and any index you have already downloaded keep working; nothing about the vault depends on the network.

Changes

If this policy changes, the "last updated" date above changes and the new version ships with the app release. There is no email list, so the app itself is the notice.

For the Play Console "Data safety" form

  • Does your app collect or share any of the required user data types? Yes — exactly one type: "Device or other IDs".
    • What: the push token (FCM registration token / OneSignal player ID) — collected by the push providers to deliver exam alerts to the device. The in-app picker narrows which exam-category topics this same token subscribes to; it does not add a data type, a second identifier, or a new recipient.
    • Sold to third parties? No.
    • Purpose: App functionality (notifications the user opted into by allowing notifications).
    • Deletion: the token is deleted when the user uninstalls the app. Turning notifications off in Android settings stops alerts being displayed on the device but does not delete the token; the in-app category and per-exam switches change which topics the token is subscribed to, not whether it exists. No other data type is collected or shared: no personal info, health, financial info, photos, location, activity, web history, or advertising ID. (Answering "No" here while FCM/OneSignal ship in the binary was the previous — wrong — answer; the push SDKs provably transmit a device-scoped token.)
  • Is all of the user data encrypted in transit? Yes (HTTPS only; usesCleartextTraffic="false").
  • Does your app provide a way for users to request that their data be deleted? Yes — Delete all data in the About sheet removes every card, setting and cached public file on the device, deleting a card removes that card, and uninstalling removes everything; the push token is deleted on uninstall.
  • Are you using a third-party analytics or advertising SDK? No.
